# Changelog

User-visible changes only.

## Unreleased

- Fixed a browser startup failure when the usage panel loaded its shared date helpers.
- Usage estimates now round half a cent up on display, including totals across agents.

- The session card now labels the action “Hide from list” and explains that new activity makes the session visible again.

- **AI Agent Office** is now released as open source under the MIT License, with the bundled cozy office and eight-species character art included in `packages/ui/art-preview` of the public repository (internal provenance notes excluded) and copy-pasteable character-set generation prompts (3×3 cut-out rig atlas + pixel-aligned garment mask) added to `docs/customizing.md`.
- Subscription limits, token counts and estimated costs now appear on the screen. The summary bar shows a compact limit chip (the highest-used window across your services, with a count when more than one exists) and today's estimated cost (`≈ $1.23 est.`, or `≥ $1.23 est.` when some tokens in the period have no price or come from an incomplete day). Clicking either chip opens the expandable "Usage & limits" section in the side panel: scrollable subscription-limit cards with reset countdowns and measurement times (dimmed after 30 minutes), and a scrollable breakdown of tokens and estimated costs for Today, 7 days or 30 days, grouped by service, project or model. Codex rollout scans now also read each turn's `model` from `turn_context` so both Claude Code and Codex tokens are priced against the bundled model price table (or your `~/.agent-office/custom-pricing.json` overrides).
- A Claude Code session that finished its turn while background work it started still runs (a command, a subagent, a monitor) now shows "Subprocesses running · N" and stays at its desk: it is not marked finished, sends no finished notice and does not go home, however long it runs. When that work ends and the turn is finished, its time counts from the first prompt, so a finished notice for a long background job is not lost. Only the number of running tasks leaves the agent's hook, never their commands.
- A subagent whose turn failed (for example on a usage limit) no longer stays in the office asking for you for days: it leaves at once and its parent session carries on. A session's own failed turn still waits for you. Failures already shown this way clear once the updated daemon is running.
- The view outside the office's window now shows how the office is doing: it rains while someone needs you, clouds over while a turn runs long, turns to night once everyone has gone home or waits in the waiting room, and stays clear while work goes well or everyone rests. Only the view changes, fading gently; the forest or sea you chose stays.
- A copy of the public repository can build and run the office: `npm run build:ui`, `npm run daemon` and `npm run ui` for the browser, `npm run app` for the desktop app. A new guide explains how to give your copy its own office and characters.
- Cats and foxes now use inclined laptop stands, with a steeper angle for the fox. Their seated posture and arm proportions stay the same.
- Colleagues sit on their chair with their feet forward (the bird keeps its feet tucked in), keeping their original shoulders and arm proportions. Working hands may hover naturally above a keyboard. When work stops, the arms no longer jump to a new angle at once; they change as the colleague gets up. Long-running work uses a sweat speech bubble above the character, readable even in a small office view, instead of a tired face.
- The office has new desks and chairs: a shallower cream desk top on four short sage legs, and a cushioned chair under each colleague. The two colleagues at a desk sit a little closer together, and the drawings and notebooks on the desks are a little smaller.
- In a larger office, the shelf and the plant beside the desks no longer stand over the first desk on the right once that part of the room opens.
- Colleagues hold their tea cup by its handle and read the paper held out in front of them with both paws.
- A colleague that has just finished its turn no longer gets up and is sent back to start again a few seconds later. When the finished turn turns into ordinary rest, it goes on with its stroll.
- With the cast set per agent in the settings (`scene.cast: per-agent`), the colleagues no longer fade in from nothing a moment after the office opens; they are drawn at once, already in place. The page now works itself out once for the updates that arrive together, instead of once for each.
- The office's walls, floors and window are drawn once and reused, instead of being drawn again on every frame; they are drawn anew when the office is resized, a room grows or shrinks, or the view outside is switched. With the office open, the desktop app used about a fifth less CPU in our measurement with 4 colleagues at their desks and about a sixth less with 12 (21 % and 16 %), and hardly less with 36 (3 %). Nothing is meant to look different; drawn with the graphics card, a few hundred pixels, nearly all along the window's bars, can come out a faint shade different.
- The office is drawn at most 30 times a second. It used to be drawn on every screen refresh, 120 times a second on a 120 Hz screen. Colleagues move at the same speed, in fewer steps. With the office open, the desktop app used about 40 to 60 % less CPU in our measurement on a 120 Hz screen (43 % to 59 % less with 4 to 36 colleagues at their desks). Clicks, hovering and updates still show at once.
- The office no longer freezes when an otter or a panda gets up from its tea. These two hold the cup in both paws; when one of them left its seat for a stroll in the middle of a sip, drawing the frame failed and the whole office stopped moving until the page was reloaded. This could happen since a routine cut short started to fade out (2026-11-05).
- OpenCode (2.x) and pi sessions can now appear in the office, with their states, tools and permission waits. Run `agent-office install-hooks opencode` or `agent-office install-hooks pi`: one small file goes into the agent's plugin folder, its settings are not changed, and only event names, tool names, the session id and the folder are passed on. Sessions of these agents cannot be stopped or restarted from the office yet.
- The schedule list can now start, stop and restart OpenClaw's and Hermes's gateways, OpenCode's background server and Codex's app-server daemon on macOS, each behind one confirmation. The office uses the tool's own command and reports only what it then sees; stop and restart wait while one of that agent's scheduled tasks is running or due within 10 minutes.
- Use Open session to manage Claude Code in its terminal. This release excludes session Stop and Restart. New session still starts Claude Code in the selected folder.

- Schedule collection now includes Claude desktop Code/Cowork, OpenClaw, Hermes and OpenCode schedulers beside Codex, with pi native scheduling explicitly unavailable. A read-only inventory shows all saved tasks and source failures. Unknown Claude recurring times and execution evidence are explicit; scheduler markers never impersonate live agent activity.

- The office has one large arched window instead of a row of windows. It fills the back wall from one side wall to the other, finished by one thin cream frame that rises from the low wall along each side wall and runs over the arch, and divided by slim wooden bars into small even panes — more panes as the office widens, the same size — and shows a calmer forest or sea in the distance, without leaves in front; the header button still switches between them. However large the office grows, the arch keeps its height and its thin frame and simply widens. Colleagues looking out stand before it in pairs along its length and rest a paw on the low wall at the foot of the glass. The office now stays clear of its header, so the header's buttons no longer cover the window or the walls (on a phone the office is a little smaller for it).
- The marker over a colleague who needs you ("! api · 승인 대기") now sits above its head instead of over its face; its bottom edge rests just above the head, on a phone too, and markers of neighbours still stack upward.
- Your phone can call you too. On the viewer (`--viewer`), a notification now shows as a banner at the top of the page with the same words as on the computer and makes your sound — the chime or the animal's cry — after you have tapped the page once; Android also vibrates, and each device can turn its sound off. Served over HTTPS by a proxy on the computer (for example `tailscale serve`; tell the daemon its name with `--viewer-host <name>`), the viewer offers "이 기기에 알림 받기": notifications then reach the phone while the page is closed or the screen is off, through the phone's own push service, encrypted so that service cannot read them, with the phone's notification sound. On an iPhone or iPad, add the page to the Home Screen and turn it on there (pair again in it). Turning a device off on the computer also stops its notifications. With the desktop app's notifications switched off (a headless server) the viewer and the phone are still notified.
- Notifications can sound like the colleague who calls. `notifications.sound: animal` plays the cry of the animal the office shows for that session (the one chosen on its card, else the one the cast gave it); the default stays a general chime, and `off` silences them. The app plays the sound itself, so it is heard even while the system blocks the banner, and several notifications at once make one sound. A notification now says what happened first, then whose it is, and names the agent, the tool and the animal: "승인 대기 · api" — "Claude Code · Bash: npm test · 고양이". While the system refuses the app's notifications the office's top bar says so, with a button to the notification settings; the tray menu's "알림 시험" shows a sample notification with your sound.
- The office board says how the month's turns went, not only how many ended. Its number is now the share of turns done without waiting for you once ("93% 혼자 끝낸 턴"); under it are the share of turns stopped (cancelled, failed or lost mid-turn), how many of your waits took more than five minutes, and the completed count. The board's records show the three shares with the counts they are made of. They are counted from the state history the daemon already keeps; days kept only as a monthly roll-up from before this change show the count alone. The monthly goal, its stamps and rewards still count completed turns.
- Working colleagues now sit naturally behind a lower desktop, with visible shoulders and connected arms instead of paws appearing through the tabletop. Tools follow the new surface height, and the shorter bird sits a little higher, including while settling into or leaving its chair.
- The office has a new, warmer look. The side walls' rim above the board and the cabinet is straighter and a little higher; furniture is cream and sage; desks have a cream top on slender legs. Windows have thin oak frames and come small (two panes) or large (three), side by side in the middle of the back wall: one large window in a small office, two large ones, then small–large–small as it grows. Behind them is one view across all the windows, a forest or the sea; the button in the office's header switches it, and the browser remembers the choice. The board has a sage frame round cream paper and the cabinet is cream with a sage back; both are larger and hang nearer the back wall, and show the same records as before. The back wall now runs straight into the side walls at the corners. Each animal works with its own tool — the cat and the fox a laptop, the otter and the hedgehog a sketchbook, the bird and the rabbit a notebook, the dog and the panda drawings — which is only for the look and says nothing about the session's work. A tool is on the desk only while its colleague sits there, so empty desks are bare.
- The board, the rewards cabinet and the windows keep their size as the office fills. They used to shrink with the room (the board by a third from 4 to 20 desks); now only the window's size and the waiting room's benches change them. The side walls they hang on are drawn larger as the room grows, and both objects are 15 % larger than before. The windows are equal frames standing on the low back wall, one, two or three of them spread along it as the office widens, instead of one large window and smaller ones in the wings standing behind the wall; while the office grows, a new window splits off and slides into place. A colleague at a window stands closer to it and rests a paw against the front of its sill. With the waiting room shown, it no longer grows when the office widens, and the office uses the space beside it; when more benches fill, the office shrinks as a whole, its board and windows with it.
- You can tell a colleague going somewhere from one strolling, and a long or silent session shows in its face. A walk to its desk for a new turn, to a bench in the waiting room or out of the door goes straight there in long steps, leaning toward it; a stroll goes two to four steps at a time, stops to look about and stand a while, and now and then ends a stop with the animal's own stretch or habit (the otter rubs its paws, the dog sniffs and tilts its head, the cat lengthens its back, the panda stretches big). A new turn or any state change cuts a stop or a routine short at once; the routine's pose fades out over a moment. A session running long works on tiredly at its laptop: a rounded back, lower head and shoulders, heavy eyelids with a blue-grey shade (the cat's ears spread, the rabbit's hang), typing between a moment with its face on a paw or loosening its wrists and neck; the clock marker and the lit laptop stay, and it is never darkened. A session with no signal sits on its bench with open eyes gazing far off and a small empty thought cloud beside it, keeping its "?" marker; it is no longer drawn faded, and it never looks asleep. Each of the eight animals walks, stops and tires in its own way.

- A colleague waiting for you raises its paw and stands in a warm light. It holds a paw up beside its cheek wherever it stands or sits (it used to raise one for two seconds in six, which reduced motion froze down for most), lowers it to walk, and has an amber glow behind its head with a pale rim around it; nothing moves. `scene.needs_you_glow: false` turns the light off. On a phone the marker now says which process waits, "! api · Claude Code", instead of the identity code.
- You can tell from across the desk who needs you and for how long. The marker over a colleague that needs you carries its name ("! api · 승인 대기"; on a phone its identity code), and the longer it waits the stronger it gets: filled, with how long, from 5 minutes, deepest from 15 (colour and size only, it never blinks or moves; `scene.needs_you_marker`). While anyone needs you the office's edge holds a still, warm light. `labels.show` chooses whose name tags show without pointing (`needs-you`, `everyone`, `on-hover`), and `scene.cast: per-project` gives every session of a project the same species.
- The screens speak English as well as Korean. The desktop window, the browser UI, the phone viewer and the desktop app's tray menu, notifications and dialogs take their words from one dictionary per language. A device whose languages do not include Korean gets English; a menu at the end of the top bar lets each device choose (remembered in that browser), and `ui.language` in `config.json` (`auto`, `ko` or `en`) sets every screen at once, the tray and notifications included. Times stay on the 24-hour clock.
- An event no longer gets lost when the machine is busy. The hook spools what it has and ends 1800 ms into its run so that its 2 s last-resort stop, which spools nothing, never comes first; the 1800 ms were counted from when the hook's code had loaded, so a slow start (seen in the tests on a busy machine) let the last-resort stop win. They now count from the hook's start.
- A session that handed work to a cloud session wears a "위임 N" chip. The Claude app can start a Claude Code cloud session from a conversation; the daemon cannot see that session, and the parent's turn ends as usual, so it looked merely at rest. Now the start is noted from the hook (the tool's name and two enums, where and who asked; never the prompt), the session shows the chip on every tab, "쉬는 중 · 클라우드 1건 돌아오길 기다림" and a ⋯ marker while it rests, and "위임한 일" on its card; when the result comes back as a task-notification prompt the delegation closes. Nothing is counted or notified: the tab and the numbers follow the session's own state. After a day without a result the chip fades.
- The office stays in view while the page scrolls. A long side panel (the list with a card open) used to scroll the office and the waiting room away with it; now only the panel moves.
- A 대기실 tab beside the list's other tabs, with the same number as the summary row's, so 전체 is the sum of the tabs beside it (나를 기다림, 작업 중, 쉬는 중, 대기실). Clicking the tab already shown, or "목록 닫기" at the panel's head, closes the whole side panel so the office fills the view; a handle on the right edge (with the needs-you number), a summary count or Esc opens it again; the choice is remembered on this device. The window's size does not change with it.
- The picture no longer shrinks for a moment when a colleague goes home. Walking to the front door took the character below the last bench row's line, which made the waiting room grow a row for the walk and both rooms scale down until it was out; a colleague on its way out asks for no row now.
- A scheduled task's thread waits in the waiting room between runs. After a run it rests at its desk for `schedule.rest_after_min` (5 minutes, so a run of a few seconds is still seen there), then sits on a bench as "예약 대기 · 다음 HH:MM", counted in 대기실; it goes home after the usual half hour unless the next run is within the hour, and comes back to the bench an hour before the next run. Ordinary sessions keep resting at their desks, since the owner may ask them anything next; a scheduled thread will not be asked until its time.
- A scheduled task's own thread is the task's figure. The first real Codex task (a "heartbeat" made by asking in a ChatGPT app thread) runs as new turns of that same thread and records no run row, only `last_run_at`; the daemon now links that thread by its exact id (`target_thread_id`) and takes `last_run_at` as the run's trace, so no occurrence is called missed after a run. In the office the thread carries the task: the "예약" chip, the "◷ HH:MM" marker while it rests, "다음 실행" on its row and the schedule at the end of its card; the bench stand-in appears only while the thread is gone home or unknown. "RRULE:" rules and minute intervals read in words ("55분마다").
- Scheduled tasks wait in the waiting room. A Codex scheduled task (the ChatGPT app's Scheduled view) whose run is within an hour (`schedule.bench_before_min`) sits on a bench as a faded colleague with its time ("◷ 09:00") and name, counted in 대기실 and 전체; its card shows the next run, the rule in words, the last run and that it is managed in the ChatGPT app. When the run starts the task leaves the bench, and the session it opened is shown as a scheduled run under the task's name (linked by the exact thread id the app records; unverified against a real run until the owner makes one). The daemon reads `~/.codex/sqlite/codex.db` read-only and says in `GET /v1/schedule` whether the file was read, empty, absent, unreadable or of an unknown shape; it never falls back to the older `codex-dev.db`. Claude Desktop's local tasks keep no schedule on disk, so their runs are marked "예약" only once they start.
- A scheduled run that never came is a needs-you entry. An occurrence that passed by `schedule.missed_after_min` (15 minutes) with no run, while the daemon was reading the schedule without a gap since before that time, is listed as "예약 놓침" under 나를 기다림 and notified (`notifications.schedule_missed`, on by default). One the daemon could not watch all the while (it, or the file, was down) is only said to be unsure on the bench and the card, never counted or notified. A later run clears either.
- The waiting room now also holds a session that has gone quiet (no signal for 15 minutes, "신호 없음"): it sits on a bench there, faded, instead of standing in the office, until its next signal or until it goes home at 30 minutes. The summary row counts the waiting room ("대기실": sessions only opened or gone quiet), so the numbers add up: 전체 = 작업 중 + 쉬는 중 + 대기실 + sessions in the office waiting for you. A quiet session that stays quiet now ends after 2 hours instead of a day (`states.unknown_gone_s`); it comes back on its next signal either way.
- A colleague's helpers are named and placed: a Codex worker or reviewer thread shows the nickname Codex gave it and its role ("Tesla · worker"), a Claude Code subagent its kind ("Explore 보조"), instead of the project folder, and in the lists a helper sits right under the session it works for, indented with "↳" and that session's name.
- "세션 열기" for a session running in VS Code (or Cursor, VSCodium) now brings forward the window that has the session's folder open, instead of only the editor's front window. The editor's own window list says which window holds the folder; when none does, the editor is brought forward as before. The Codex panel or terminal tab inside that window still has to be picked by hand.
- A subagent that goes quiet no longer stands in the office with a "?" for a day. A colleague's helper (a Claude Code subagent, a Codex worker or reviewer thread) whose end was never reported leaves after 15 minutes of silence (`states.working_stale_s`) like one that finished, and comes back by itself if it reports again. Helpers that are still answering stay as before.
- A Codex approval that Codex answers itself no longer shows as waiting for you. Codex's auto-review ("Approve for me") decides escalations in a second but still fires the permission hook first, so the office showed "승인 대기" and notified while the command was already running. The daemon now reads who answers the turn's approvals from the session's own rollout (two settings keys, nothing else) and keeps such a session working, with the command on its tool line; a thread you review yourself still waits and notifies as before.
- Hooks survive a Node upgrade. `install-hooks` now points the hook launcher at Homebrew's `opt` link for Node instead of the versioned folder that an upgrade removes (on 2026-10-31 one did, and the office showed nothing new for sixteen hours). When the launcher's Node is gone all the same, a Node 22.18 or later on the agent's path runs the hook, and `agent-office doctor` asks you to run `install-hooks` again. The office's top bar now says when hooks are not reaching the daemon ("훅이 데몬에 닿지 않아요" or "훅 설정이 깨졌어요"), with the command to run, and the notice goes away on its own once hooks arrive again.
- The number on the "퇴근" tab now counts the sessions the conditions under it let through, so it matches the rows below (it had counted every session that left, while a remembered "오늘" showed only today's). Searching still narrows the rows only.
- A waiting room below the office, with benches, a coat rack and a plant, joined to the office by a door and with a front door out. A session that was only opened sits on a bench there ("출근 대기", reading the paper or having tea) until its first prompt, when it walks out through the door and in through the office's to its desk. A session going home — away after a long rest, sent home with "목록에서 숨기기", or ended — gets up, walks to the waiting room, sits a moment and leaves by the front door; only then does it drop off the office and show on "퇴근" alone. The room grows a row of benches at a time when more sessions wait. "대기실 끄기" on the office hides it (remembered on this computer): the office alone fills the view as before, nobody waits on screen, and a session leaving fades out where it is.
- The office's walls, floor and windows are the study's new pictures: cream plaster walls with a low wooden skirting and a door in the front wall, a plank floor laid in staggered boards instead of stretched, and oak windows with a pale sky and distant rooftops outside. The board and the rewards cabinet hang a little lower and larger on the side walls. The plants and the shelf stand along the walls, the sofa sits lower on the planks, and the wings' desks and coffee corners moved a little; the room is a little wider.
- A session's card keeps a fixed line for its tool: during a turn it names the tool running now (Bash, Edit, … with its program or file) or says the agent is answering without one; while the session waits for you it names what the approval or question is about; at rest it says none. The line no longer appears and disappears as tools come and go, so the card's facts stay put. The hover preview says the same.
- The All tab now counts who is in the office: a session that was only opened (an app reopening an old conversation, nothing asked yet) waits in the waiting room (see below), not at a desk or a rest seat, since you can still go to its window and give it a command or close it; its first prompt seats it. If nothing is asked for 30 minutes (`states.away_after_min`) it goes home like a resting colleague and the "퇴근" tab says "퇴근함 · 열리기만 함". Colleagues gone home are no longer counted on the All tab, only on "퇴근". Threads the Codex app runs for itself ("앱 내부 작업") are hidden from the All tab unless you tick "앱 내부 작업 보기" above the list (remembered on this computer); they are never in the office.
- Each animal's clothes now have their own details, in the session's colour: the cat's argyle knit vest with wooden buttons, the otter's canvas pocket apron, the bird's check scarf, the dog's work jacket with flap pockets, the rabbit's flower-embroidered cardigan, the fox's herringbone vest, the panda's cable-knit cardigan and the hedgehog's check pocket vest, in the office and on the cards' portraits. The details are drawn over the clothes, so they move with the body; there are no new images.
- Smaller steps and each animal's own way of sitting: walking paws keep their drawn size and round shape (legs no longer stretch and shrink with each step), the two paws alternate properly when walking up or down the room, and a colleague turning to face you keeps its paws where they are while its picture changes. On the sofa and the stools each animal rests its own way (the otter and the rabbit stretch their feet out, the panda shows its soles, the cat and the hedgehog tuck short feet in, the dog and the fox sit with their feet apart) and the bird tucks its feet under its belly; sitting down, standing up and a sit cut short take a quarter longer, so the feet keep step. At the window a colleague rests a free paw on the sill; one carrying tea keeps its cup and just looks out.

Resting colleagues sit properly on the sofa and the stools, legs bent, and their name tag goes up with them; they no longer drop to the floor for a moment when they settle again on the seat they already had. The sofa and the coffee corners are further from the desks, and colleagues walk around furniture and plants instead of through them (walks to the lounge take a little longer).
- A "퇴근" tab beside the office lists who left: colleagues gone home after a long rest, ones you sent home, sessions their agent ended and ones whose process stopped mid-turn, latest first, each saying how its last turn ended (정상 완료, Esc로 중단, 취소됨, 실패, 작업 중 종료). Conditions above the list narrow it by agent (Claude Code, Codex), by when the session left (1시간, 2시간, 오늘) and to those worth a look (failed or stopped mid-turn, the two kinds that never leave on their own); they are remembered on this computer. - Threads the Codex desktop app runs for itself (it starts short threads of its own, for memory upkeep and suggestions, that send the same events as a conversation but have no conversation to open) no longer stand in the office or count as needing you; the All tab lists them as "앱 내부 작업", and "세션 열기" no longer sends the Codex app to a thread it cannot find ("Failed to resume chat"). A thread is told apart by Codex's own thread list, read read-only, a few seconds after it first appears.
- The office now shows only what may need you: colleagues at work, waiting for you, failed, and those that finished or went quiet in the last 30 minutes. After that they go home on their own ("퇴근함" on the All tab) and come back with their next turn; `states.away_after_min` in `config.json` changes the 30 minutes (0 keeps them). Sessions are named by the title their own app shows (Claude Code's session title, a Codex thread's name) unless you gave them a nickname, so you can tell which conversation it is; titles are hidden in screen-share mode and from paired phones. For a session in the Claude or Codex desktop app, "세션 열기" now tries to open that conversation in the app instead of only bringing the app forward. "목록에서 숨기기" now sits right under "세션 열기" on the card. After the daemon has been stopped for a while, hooks it missed are replayed in the order they happened, so a finished turn no longer comes back as "working".
- Esc in Claude Code now shows: a turn you interrupted, or a request you turned down, with Esc ends as cancelled and the colleague goes to rest within about 40 seconds, instead of staying "working" or "waiting for you" until your next prompt. Claude Code sends no event for Esc, so agent office looks at the end of that session's own transcript for the line Claude Code writes on Esc, only after the turn has been quiet for 10 seconds; it compares that one line with Claude Code's fixed marker and keeps only its time.
- Calmer states: a colleague whose turn just ended no longer gets up for a moment only to sit back down; finished, cancelled and paused sessions all rest like any resting one and show the finished check at once. A session that has gone quiet for 15 minutes is now only marked with a quiet "?" and no longer counted as needing you or notified (most such turns were long commands that came back by themselves); you can send it home. A session still showing an approval request you denied with Esc in Claude Code can be sent home too.
- Send a colleague home: a session's card on the computer has "목록에서 숨기기" for a session that is resting, finished, failed, paused or has gone quiet. It leaves the office, the lists and the needs-you list; the agent itself is not touched, and the session comes back by itself as soon as it does anything again. Sessions that were only opened (an app reopening old sessions after a restart, a session nobody has asked anything yet) are listed on the All tab as "열리기만 함" and come into the office with their first prompt. The list and cards now say where a session runs: Claude app, Codex app, VS Code or a terminal.
- The office has a small wooden board on its left wall and a cabinet of monthly rewards on its right wall. The board shows how many turns finished this month and today and how many sessions need a look now; click it for the month or quarter with a chart, and the cabinet for the year's rewards and each month's record. Days with no record stay blank instead of zero. There is no goal by default; set `scene.board.monthly_target` in `config.json` to collect 25/50/75/100% stamps and a plaque for each month that reaches it (a past month keeps the goal it had). `scene.board.preset` picks what the board shows (`combined`, `goal`, `rhythm`, `health`), `scene.board.rewards` shows them on a shelf, in frames or not at all, and `scene.board.show: false` takes the board down. Turns you cancelled show on a line of their own in the records and never count toward the goal. When the office grows, its diagonal side walls now move out past the new windows, so the board and the rewards stay in view. Only on the computer, not on paired phones.
- Each session now gets its own animal from eight (otter, cat, bird, dog, rabbit, fox, panda, hedgehog), so several sessions of the same agent look different; the same session keeps its animal on every screen and after reconnecting. Pick another one on a session's card ("캐릭터"; kept with its note, phones see it too). `scene.cast: "per-agent"` in `config.json` brings back one animal per agent kind, following `species` (cat for Claude Code, otter for Codex by default).
- The office has small routines: at its laptop a working colleague types with its paws on the keys, reads, thinks with its chin in its paw, jots a note or stretches; at a rest seat, its own included, it reads a newspaper, drinks tea, daydreams, yawns, nods or stretches; at the window it gazes out; in a conversation the two greet, talk, listen and laugh; and a colleague carrying its tea may stop on a stroll for a sip. A colleague going back to work turns to its laptop before it sits down, and only then starts typing. Pointing at a colleague or opening its card keeps it in place but lets it finish turning or sitting down and go on with its routine. Desks and laptops are drawn as separate pieces; the room sees a laptop's back lid, whose small power light is on during a turn. Five more species (dog, rabbit, fox, panda, hedgehog) can be drawn with all of these.
- See at a glance who is working and who is resting: the summary row and the list now count and list working and resting sessions on their own tabs (작업 중, 쉬는 중), and clicking a count opens its tab. Sessions whose turn finished and sessions that were opened or cleared without a turn are both "쉬는 중"; the former keep their check and the time the turn ended. In the office only colleagues in a turn sit at desks, with their laptop on: a resting colleague never sits at a desk (when the rest seats are full it stands or moves about, even with its card open), and one whose turn starts walks briskly back to its desk, whose laptop is already on. Colleagues waiting on their own subagent or tool stay at their desks.
- The office comes alive: colleagues that finished a turn or are paused get up and stroll, rest, look out of the window or chat with each other; those waiting for you, waiting on another task or resting do the same after five minutes (`scene.wander_after_s` in `config.json`, 0 for at once), and a waiting one keeps its "!" and raises a paw now and then while it moves. Working colleagues stay at their desks and walk back when work resumes. Walking, turning and sitting down look steadier. Reduced motion and the pause keep everyone still.
- Name your sessions: on a card (on the computer) give a session a nickname, a one-line memo, a star ("지켜보기": listed first) and "쉬기 시작하면 알림", which notifies you when that session finishes a turn even if finished turns are not notified otherwise. The office and the list show the nickname and the star; the memo shows only on the card. Phones and tablets see the nickname and star but cannot change them. "크게 보기" lets the office fill the window (full screen when the browser allows), with cards opening over it; Esc brings the list back.
- Watch the office from a phone, tablet or desk screen on your Wi-Fi: start the daemon with `--viewer <this computer's Wi-Fi address>` (or `npm run app -- --viewer <address>`), run `agent-office pair` (or "기기 연결…" in the tray menu) and type the code on the device. The device can only look: it sees names, states and who is waiting for you, never folders or (by default) commands, and its cards say which computer to go to and what to run there. `agent-office devices` lists paired devices and `devices revoke <id>` (or the tray menu) cuts one off at once. On a plain Wi-Fi address the traffic is not encrypted; the daemon says so when it starts.
- Open session (macOS): a session's card has one button that takes you to it. A running session's iTerm2 tab, Terminal tab or tmux pane comes forward (otherwise the app it runs in); a session whose process is gone opens again with its own resume command (`claude --resume`, `codex resume`) in a new window of the terminal it ran in. Clicking a notification does the same and leaves the card, with what happened, behind the terminal. Nothing is approved or typed into the session. macOS asks once per terminal app whether agent office may control it. On Linux and Windows, and for sessions reached over ssh, the card shows the command to copy instead.
- A desktop app for macOS, for developers (`npm run app`): it opens the office in a window, starting the daemon if none is running (and stopping it again on quit), shows the number of sessions that need you in the menu bar, and notifies you when a session waits for your approval or input for more than 3 seconds (15 for SDK runs), fails, goes silent or exits mid-turn. Clicking a notification opens that session's card; nothing is approved from the app. Finished turns are notified only with `notifications.ready` on. What was already waiting when the daemon started is listed, not notified. The tray menu pauses notifications for an hour or until you turn them on again. In screen-share mode notifications leave out project names and tool targets. Closing the window keeps the app in the menu bar. If macOS refuses the app's notifications, the tray menu says so and opens the notification settings.
- A browser view of your sessions: run `npm run build:ui` once, start the daemon, then `agent-office ui`, which prints a link to open within a minute (it works once). The page shows an office where each session is a colleague (a cat for Claude Code, an otter for Codex), beside a list with the sessions that need you, those that finished and all of them, with search. Markers show who waits for you, failed, went silent or has been running long; pointing at a colleague or a row shows a small card, and a click pins the details. Nothing can be approved or answered from the page. The office grows as sessions start, and every working session gets a desk. Reduced motion stops the scene; the list keeps updating. The page can only read: it cannot report events.
- The daemon serves an attention list at `/v1/attention`, and as `attention` events on `/v1/stream` whenever it changes: sessions that need you (approval or input waits, failed turns, sessions that fell silent, and sessions whose agent process exited mid-turn), sessions that finished a turn and wait for the next instruction, and turns running longer than `states.long_turn_min` (default 20 minutes, not counting time spent waiting for you). Nothing is notified yet.
- First runnable parts, for developers (Node 22.18 or later, run from a checkout): the hook program (`agent-office-hook`) that strips each hook event to metadata and sends it to the local daemon or spools it, and a headless daemon (`agent-office daemon`) that keeps session state in SQLite and serves it on 127.0.0.1 with a token. A session's `detail` shows the tool with its program or file name; the description the model writes for a shell command is shown only with `privacy.detail_level: description`. `agent-office install-hooks <claude-code|codex>` adds the hooks to Claude Code's or Codex's settings on macOS and Linux after showing the exact changes and asking (`--dry-run` only shows them), keeps a backup of the previous file, and changes nothing else; `uninstall-hooks` removes exactly those entries. Codex runs new hooks only after you trust them with `/hooks`. Windows is not supported yet. `agent-office emit` lets scripts and cron jobs report their own state (working, waiting, done, failed) as a character; it waits for the daemon when the daemon is not running. The daemon reads token usage from Claude Code and Codex logs and Codex's rate limits (allowlisted fields only; recent files are read from the end, and transcripts then forward from where the last read stopped) and serves them at `/v1/usage` and `/v1/limits`; costs stay empty until a price table ships. Claude Code sessions started by a schedule or the SDK are marked as such, from the session's own transcript metadata. On macOS and Linux a session ends when its agent process exits, even without a final hook. `agent-office doctor` checks the setup (daemon, hooks, launcher, spool, recent hook errors); `--drift` also checks that your own agent logs still parse, printing counts only.
